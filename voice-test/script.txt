Take off your glasses, or your sunglasses, and look at the name on the side. Ray-Ban. Oakley. Persol. Maybe Prada, Chanel or Armani. Now think about where you bought them. Maybe LensCrafters, or Sunglass Hut, or Pearle Vision. Maybe your vision insurance helped pay. It feels like a choice between dozens of rival brands and stores. It isn't. A huge share of that path, the frame, the lens, the store and even the insurance, leads back to one company. It's called EssilorLuxottica. In 2024 it brought in twenty-six and a half billion euros. And it was built by a man who grew up in an orphanage in Milan. This is the story of how he did it, how he crushed a rival that dared to say no, and why your glasses cost what they cost. To understand it, we have to go back to 1935. Leonardo Del Vecchio was born in Milan in May 1935, to a poor family from the south of Italy. His father, a street vendor who sold vegetables, died before he was born.
